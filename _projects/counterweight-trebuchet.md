*"Why would anyone build a trebuchet?"*

A few friends of mine asked, and were asked, this question while we built this medieval
trebuchet, which was capable of launching a 15 lb. projectile around 270 ft.

![Counterweight trebuchet](/assets/images/counterweight-trebuchet.png)

## Background

The trebuchet is a large artillery weapon which traces its origins to 12th century Europe. It was
largely deployed in siege warfare to launch heavy projectiles against enemy fortifications. The
trebuchet was made obsolete by gunpowder artillery, and saw its last recorded deployment in 1521.

The counterweight trebuchet is capable of throwing projectiles at very high velocities due to a
few critical design components. Briefly, the trebuchet converts the potential energy of the
counterweight, raised above the ground, into kinetic energy. When the counterweight falls, it
applies a torque to the arm, lifting the sling and the projectile. Finally, the sling whips about
the end of the arm, releasing the projectile at a high initial velocity and height.

## Design Iterations

For this project, we were constrained to conventional lumber sizes and a $300 budget. I led the
CAD design of the trebuchet and utilized Fusion 360 to obtain dimensions for the cuts.

The frame features two triangular supports, and a track for the projectile. The most notable
feature of the frame is the use of 3 boards to create the vertical supports, which allowed the
arm's axle to rest atop the supports for easy removal.

Additionally, I designed the counterweight jockey to be compatible with Olympic fitness weights.
This allowed for easy and accurate testing with different counterweight values.
